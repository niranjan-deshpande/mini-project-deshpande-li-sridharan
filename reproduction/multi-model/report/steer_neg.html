T3b · Steering against the direction (GLM-4.5-Air)

Does pushing GLM away from the hack direction stop it hacking? For each of the 84 impossible-split rollouts in which GLM carried out a hack, we cut the transcript just before the first turn that carries it out and regenerated that turn twice per condition. Steering adds −k·v (span vector, layer 33, as in T3) at every position, inside vLLM on the same FP8 model that produced the rollouts. The controls are no steering and a random direction of the same norm as 3×v. The GPT-5.6 Sol judge labels each regenerated turn: any hack (considered or carried out), and what the turn mainly does.

No: steering against the direction does not reduce hacking. Unsteered, GLM re-hacks at the cut point 32% of the time (carried out 26%). Otherwise it makes an honest attempt or flags the contradictory tests, so there is room to move. At −1× and −1.5×, where the model stays mostly intact, the hack rate does not move (+0 pts [-8, +8] and +2 pts [-5, +10]). The intervals exclude a drop of more than about 8 points.

The apparent drop at −2× is breakage. Overall hacking falls by -11 pts [-20, -2], but 43% of outputs are incoherent and 43% run to the 8,192-token cap. Among the turns that stay coherent, GLM hacks 35% of the time, the same as unsteered (33%). At −3× almost nothing coherent is left. Breaking the model generically does not help either: the random direction leaves overall hacking unchanged and raises it among coherent turns to 53%.

Reading. This matches the rest of the GLM results: steering toward v did not raise contemplation (T3), and the probe is weakest on carried-out hacks (T2b). The direction reads out hack-related deliberation, but it is not a lever on GLM's behaviour.
