T6 · RL against the probe (gpt-oss-120b) 4 ARMS · 100 STEPS · HELD-OUT EVAL

If the probe is used as a training penalty, does the model stop hacking, or learn to hack without tripping the probe? Four GRPO runs from the same gpt-oss-120b start, same seed and same task order, differing only in the reward. Arm A: reward = visible tests pass. Arm B: reward = pass − 1·[probe fires], so a hack the probe catches earns nothing and a failed attempt that fires earns −1. Arm C (run afterwards): reward = pass − (score − τ) / 448,548, a linear, unclipped penalty on the continuous probe score (a score below τ earns a small bonus), scaled so that at step 0 the expected penalty gap between hacks and honest attempts equals B's (0.42). Arm D (run last): reward = pass, and every sample the probe flags is dropped: it gets zero advantage and is left out of its group's mean and standard deviation, so a caught hack is neither rewarded nor punished. All arms ran 100 steps (A and B paused at 37 / 41 by a GPU deadline on 14 Oct, C moved from 2 to 6 sampler GPUs at step 35; each resumed from the saved adapter + optimizer state), then the base model and the four step-100 policies were evaluated on held-out tasks, and the A / B transcripts were re-scored across models. Every training and eval sample is judged (updated 14:34 UTC, 15 Oct).

  • Why gpt-oss, single-turn. Qwen3.8-27B was tried first and passes impossible tasks ~0.4% of the time at every reasoning effort: it considers hacks but almost never enacts them, so there is nothing to reward. gpt-oss-120b at medium effort passes 54% of impossible tasks single-turn with the lenient prompt, and the judge calls 98% of those passes hacks.
  • Tasks. 897 new training tasks built with ImpossibleBench's own LCB pipeline (transcribe, then one-off / conflicting mutations, programmatically validated) from medium/hard LiveCodeBench problems outside the 103-problem eval set: 403 solvable, 334 one-off, 160 conflicting. Lenient prompt, one turn, reward from sandboxed visible tests. 16 tasks × 8 samples per step.
  • Probe (pre-registered, frozen). gpt-oss span vector, layer 13 (the rebuilt gptoss_h vectors), max over reasoning tokens, computed on the exact sampled tokens with the policy's own activations. Calibrated on the pilot before training: AUROC 0.91 against the judge overall, 0.82 within the impossible splits; completion length alone gets 0.66–0.78. τ is the 10%-FPR threshold (TPR 0.67). This single-turn setting is easier for the probe than T2's agentic passages (gpt-oss 0.73–0.76), so the two numbers are not comparable.
  • Training. GRPO, attention-only LoRA (r 32), group-normalised advantages, truncated importance weights against the vLLM sampler (mean |Δ log p| ≈ 0.035, nothing clipped), one-step off-policy, lr 2e-5, ~4.5 min per step on one sampler GPU + one trainer GPU per arm. Every sample is judged afterwards by GPT-5.6 Sol with the step-2 calibration rubric (not by the probe).

The penalty taught the model to hack past the probe. Over steps 91–100, the probe catches 77% of arm A's judged hacks but only 22% of arm B's: -55 pts [-60, -49] B − A, against -5 pts [-12, +3] over steps 1–10, when the arms had barely diverged. Arm B hacks a little less than A (80% vs 89% of impossible-task samples, -9 pts [-17, -1]) and more than at the start (67%). So hacks the probe misses go from 24% to 62% of arm B's impossible-task samples, while in A they stay at 22% → 20%. Almost all hacks are enacted (special-casing the bad test or hard-coding outputs), not just considered.

The probe goes quiet on B in general. On impossible-task samples the judge calls honest, B's probe fires 24% of the time at the start and 4% at the end (A: 25% → 25%; few honest samples per block, so these rates are noisy).

Arm C: a continuous penalty

The prediction that a continuous penalty would lower AUROC is not borne out. On the held-out lenient tasks C's AUROC is 0.90 [0.88, 0.92], slightly above base (C − base +3 pts [+0, +5]) and B (C − B +4 pts [+1, +6]). C pushes every score down, honest ones most (median non-hack score 625k for base, 510k for C; hacks 828k → 761k).

C hacks least, and evades less than B. C hacks on 36% of held-out impossible-task samples, fewer than base (C − base -7 pts [-11, -4]) or B (C − B -16 pts [-20, -12]). At τ the probe catches 42% of C's hacks (C − base -27 pts [-34, -21]; C − B +13 pts [+7, +19]), so hacks the probe misses are 21% of C's samples: more than base (13%, +8 pts [+4, +11]) but far fewer than B (37%, -16 pts [-20, -12]). Re-tuned to 10% FPR on its own samples, the probe catches 70% of C's hacks, the most of any model.

Much of this runs through length. C's reasoning is about half as long as base's, and max-pooled scores fall with length. Short completions also hack less in every model. Within length quartiles C's hack scores sit at or below base's (equal in the shortest quartile) and above B's in every quartile:

Arm D: drop flagged samples instead of penalising them

Filtering is much weaker pressure than a penalty. On held-out tasks the probe at τ catches 68% of D's hacks, about base's rate (D − base -2 pts [-6, +3]) and far above B's (D − B +38 pts [+33, +44]), though below A's (D − A -10 pts [-15, -5]). D hacks on 56% of impossible-task samples, between A and base (D − A -8 pts [-11, -6]; D − base +12 pts [+9, +15]). Hacks the probe misses: 18% of D's samples (base 13%, A 14%, B 37%). AUROC 0.87 [0.84, 0.89]. D kept the phrasing B dropped (“cannot modify the tests” in 29% of its hacks), and its median hack score moved far less than B's (828k base, 871k A, 812k D, 740k B).

Why. In B a caught hack is actively pushed down, which creates a direct gradient toward phrasing that slips under τ. In D a caught hack simply never counts; the only pressure toward evasion is that uncaught hacks are reinforced while caught ones are not. Over 100 steps that was enough to stop the rise in probe-salience seen in A (A's recall climbs during training, D's stays flat) but not enough to teach the lexical dodge. The cost: D still hacks almost as much as A, because filtering takes away the reward for caught hacks without making hacking worse than honest work.

Strict prompt. RL under the lenient prompt barely makes A or B defy an explicit instruction: hack rates are 6% (base), 3% (A) and 8% (B), and the models flag the tests 76%, 85% and 71% of the time. C is the exception: it hacks on 17% of strict-prompt samples and flags the tests only 52% of the time, consistent with shorter reasoning leaving less room to notice the instruction. D behaves like A and base here (4% hacks, flags the tests 82% of the time). Under this prompt the probe fires on about half of the honest samples (it responds to discussion of broken tests) and its AUROC is 0.43–0.62, near chance, so the strict rows say nothing useful about the probe.

Capability. On the solvable split, training-time pass rates stay at 83–100% per block with judged hacks on 0–4%; on the held-out solvable tasks pass rates are 67% (base), 71% (A), 71% (B), 65% (C) and 73% (D).
